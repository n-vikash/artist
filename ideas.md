# Birthday Gallery Design Direction

## Three Initial Directions

| Theme Name | Very Brief Intro | Probability |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **After-Hours Sketchbook** | A dark, tactile studio notebook where graphite marks, torn paper, and restrained color create a grown-up but mischievous art world. | 0.07 |
| **Museum Mischief** | A minimalist gallery interrupted by playful curator notes, unexpected captions, and surreal moments of celebration. | 0.04 |
| **Paper Constellation** | An airy cosmic collage where artwork travels through paper cut-outs and quietly glowing fragments. | 0.08 |

## Chosen Direction: After-Hours Sketchbook

### Design Movement

**Editorial collage meets an analog artist’s notebook.** The experience should feel as though a funny friend raided a serious creative studio after midnight and turned the best pages into a one-night exhibition.

### Core Principles

The interface uses asymmetry, material texture, and visual restraint around the artwork. Motion has narrative purpose: it reveals a new page, rewards curiosity, or briefly lands a joke. Controls are intentionally direct and tactile, while decorative marks never compete with the art. The story stays plainly friendship-first—playful, admiring, and slightly chaotic without sentimental romance.

### Color Philosophy

The baseline is **graphite and inky navy**, creating a quiet gallery environment that makes supplied artwork visually dominant. **Butter yellow** acts as an optimistic mark of celebration, while muted **aqua** and **coral** appear as rare paint-flick accents. This uses color as an interruption rather than wallpaper.

### Layout Paradigm

The site works as a **continuous stage notebook**, not a conventional page. The viewport is the exhibit wall; copy attaches to the edges, toolbar controls live on a movable paper strip, and the artwork occupies a shifting central mat. Sections crossfade as scenes, rather than forming a stack of equally shaped cards.

### Signature Elements

The system repeats three motifs: an off-register hand-drawn circular pencil line; torn paper labels with a small “archive” tab; and tiny, sparse star/brush/pencil doodles that drift only at the edges. The Loopmark, a continuous imperfect brushstroke burst, is the persistent visual anchor.

### Interaction Philosophy

Interactions should feel like handling a physical sketchbook: buttons depress, pages nudge, artwork comes forward, and modal work enlarges without disorientation. Users always receive a clear next action and never need hover alone to understand a control.

### Animation

Scene transitions use opacity, transform, and masking cues that suggest ink settling or paper sliding. Artwork changes rotate among a soft shift, a biased page slide, and a restrained blur-to-focus reveal; they never happen faster than reading speed. The final celebration has a single controlled confetti moment. All non-essential movement stops or simplifies under `prefers-reduced-motion`.

### Typography System

**DM Serif Display** provides high-contrast editorial drama for key words and name reveals. **Space Grotesk** keeps controls, counters, and friendly side-notes precise. Display type is large and often left-offset; UI labels use tracked uppercase to evoke archival labeling without becoming sterile.

### Brand Essence

**An animated after-hours art exhibition made by a funny friend for an artist’s birthday, designed to turn 32 drawings into a story.** The personality is **witty, tactile, and observant**.

### Brand Voice

Headlines are short, theatrical, and knowingly undercut by a later line. CTAs sound like invitations to a bit, not product funnels. Examples: “**Please don’t judge the website.**” and “**This is where the dramatic art critic part begins.**” Generic greetings and romantic language are excluded.

### Wordmark & Logo

The wordmark is a compact **“After Hours / 32 Pages”** archive label paired with the custom Loopmark: an imperfect graphite loop with one yellow dot and aqua flick. The mark contains no lettering and works independently as the header and favicon icon.

### Signature Brand Color

**Pencil-Sun #F6D96D** is the ownable birthday accent: warm, papery, and deliberately sparing against the dark field.

## Style Decisions

Celebration graphics remain secondary to the studio-notebook language: confetti, cake, stars, and cut-paper shapes are sparse edge interruptions rather than dominant backdrops. Every scene carries at least one tactile notebook cue—torn paper, a graphite loop, a pinned mat, an archive tab, a pencil smudge, or a taped label—so it never drifts into a generic dark party treatment. While the personal assets are pending, the interface uses neutral private-archive cues rather than visible bracket variables.

## Current User Direction — Pastel Birthday Scrapbook

The active visual direction is now **pastel pink, white, sky blue, lavender, and silver** with a cute premium birthday atmosphere. Balloons, ribbons, stars, sparkles, confetti, cake, candle flames, fireworks, crackers, and scrapbook-style frames are welcome as celebratory motifs, provided the result remains friendship-first and non-romantic. Handwritten accents may appear alongside the elegant display serif and clean UI sans. The art gallery stays the narrative center, with pink/white/blue frames and gentle page-placement motion around each supplied drawing.

This user-requested direction supersedes the earlier dark palette for the current revision.
